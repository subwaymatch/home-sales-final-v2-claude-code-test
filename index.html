Data and methods

Source

College-town home sales dataset, version 1.0 (2026). University of Illinois-Deloitte Foundation Center for Business Emerging Technologies. Data collected March 2026. One row per completed sale, with the list, contract and sale dates, the price, the home's attributes and its tax. Values are as reported by the collection source and were not checked against county records.

Column definitions and known problems are in docs/data_description_sheet.md. The file is data/college_town_home_sales.csv. The same data is also available as Excel, SQLite, DuckDB and Parquet files in the data/ folder.

Rules used

  • Valid price: a sale price from $10,000 to $5,000,000, inclusive. Used everywhere except Q2, Q3 and Q9, which use all rows as their questions state. Sales outside the range are removed from medians and counts, not from the dataset.
  • Days on market: contract date minus list date, in days, for rows with both dates. A blank date is not zero, so rows without both dates are left out of days-on-market figures. The list date is the listing that led to the sale; a sale that fell through and was re-listed keeps its original list date.
  • Year and month: from the sale date. Rows with a blank sale date (6 in the file) are left out of any year-based result.
  • City: city and state together, such as "Champaign, IL". Campus area: the university column, which is the nearest campus to each home.
  • Map position: each city is placed at the median latitude and longitude of its sales.
  • Median unless a question asks for a mean. Ties are reported in full.
  • Rounding: dollars to whole dollars; days, miles and percents to one decimal; half up.
  • Duplicate addresses: rows are never removed because two sales share a street address. listing_id is the key.

How missing values are handled

  • Home features (garage, basement, fireplace): a blank means unknown, not "no". A filter set to "Has" or "No" leaves out homes whose value is blank. Blank basement and fireplace values are common, so those filters show fewer sales than the full set.
  • Living area: blank and zero values are treated as unknown. When the living-area range is narrowed, those homes are left out; at the full range they stay in.
  • Bedrooms, tax and distance: a blank value is left out of the calculations that need it. A bedroom filter leaves out homes with a blank bedroom count.
  • Annual tax (Q6): rows with a blank tax, a tax of $0 or less, or a tax at or above the sale price are left out.

What the filters do

  • The Explore filters, the map, the results table and the comparison use the valid price rule and the filters you set.
  • Q1 to Q6 always use the full dataset with the rules stated in each section. The Explore filters do not change them.
  • Q7 to Q10 keep their own controls and use the full dataset.
  • The filter settings are stored in the page address, so a copied link opens the same view.
